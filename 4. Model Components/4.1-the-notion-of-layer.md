# Chapter 4 - Model Components

A deep model can be understood as a _complex_ combination of _standard_ mathematical operations. As such, the complexity appears from how the architecture is created, how information flows, how specific modules of the architecture function on their own _and_ interact with other modules, etc. Broken down, however, atomic components are usually standard mathematical operations, as evident throughout the book.

The field has developed specific components of deep architectures that have clear semantics, or _roles_ to play, in a deep model. The modular nature of deep architectures, as discussed before, allows for a _plug-and-play_ approach. This approach allows for _specific_ architectures suited for _specific_ domains and/or _specific_ tasks. Most flows in the book keep reinforcing the importance of inductive bias at _all_ levels in a deep architecture, and _rightfully_ so. It is hard to overstate the unique power of inductive bias, supercharged by modularization, in deep learning.

# 4.1 The notion of layer
The book describes layers as _“standard complex compounded tensor operations that have been designed and empirically identified as being generic and efficient.”_ Deep learning, at its core, is a successive series of compounding operations on an input signal. A layer _standardizes_ the compounding processes at a specific level of granularity, such that all/most compounding at a layer’s level is consistent, operations are applied consistently across the input signal at the layer’s level, the inductive biases at the layer’s level are consistent, and the output from the layer for the next one is consistent. A layer’s architecture, including the trainable parameters it may incorporate and the hyperparameters that are chosen for it, applies _consistently_ to the incoming signal. Consequently, layers provide a convenient level of detail for building and describing deep models.

Layers are the _building blocks_ of deep architectures. It becomes apparent throughout the book that understanding and interpreting architectures through the lens of layers is very convenient. An architecture can, for example, be _an embedding layer followed by an attention layer followed by a fully connected layer_.
