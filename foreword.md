# Foreword

Deep learning really started to kick off after it was shown that it scales remarkably well. And that it could outperform current state-of-the-art models _by virtue of just the scale_, both of data and of architecture. GPUs made scaling possible because their parallelized design was able to actually handle it. Essentially, scaling data, model, and compute together unlocks dramatic improvements in performance.

This led to a wave of research in deep learning in the previous decade and consequent innovations in:
•	Structure (new layers, new connection paths, etc.) 
•	Training strategies (Adam, etc.)
•	Hardware (GPU improvements, etc.)

The complexity in deep learning comes from the interplay and combination of several fields. Individually, they are _somewhat_ lucid. Some are even taught in high school. DL is an interplay between algebra, calculus, trigonometry, probability theory, programming, software engineering, etc. among other things. The complexity _(and the beauty)_ comes from how concepts from each of these fields are employed in isolation _and_ in conjunction with each other.

Deep learning presents itself to me as a masterstroke in modularity. Specifically, the concepts are presented in this book with such elegance that multiple careful readings combined with a Gen AI learning companion helped “bring out” individual components and complete architectures as abstractions in front of the eyes. It puts to rest any doubt (at least in my brain) on why deep learning has become the face of modern machine learning. Not to take away anything from more “conventional” machine learning models – there’s actually overlap.
